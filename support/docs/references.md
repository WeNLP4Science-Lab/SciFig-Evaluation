## Section 8: Metric Definition Methodology

51. **BERTScore** — Zhang, T., Kishore, V., Wu, F., Weinberger, K.Q. & Artzi, Y. (2020). "BERTScore: Evaluating Text Generation with BERT." *ICLR 2020.* arXiv:1904.09675. — Exemplar of rigorous metric definition: formally defines precision/recall/F1 over contextual embeddings, validates via correlation with human judgments across 363 MT and captioning systems, and demonstrates robustness via adversarial paraphrase detection. **Relevance:** Model for how to define token-level similarity metrics with clear mathematical formulation and extensive human-correlation validation.

52. **CLIPScore** — Hessel, J., Holtzman, A., Forbes, M., Le Bras, R. & Choi, Y. (2021). "CLIPScore: A Reference-free Evaluation Metric for Image Captioning." *EMNLP 2021.* arXiv:2104.08718. — Demonstrates that cross-modal pretrained models (CLIP) can serve as reference-free evaluation metrics, achieving highest correlation with human judgments for image-caption pairs. **Relevance:** Precedent for defining metrics that evaluate vision-language alignment without requiring ground-truth references, analogous to our Resistance metric evaluating whether model output aligns with visual evidence vs. misleading context.

53. **FActScore** — Min, S., Krishna, K., Lyu, X., Lewis, M., Yih, W., Koh, P.W., Iyyer, M., Zettlemoyer, L. & Hajishirzi, H. (2023). "FActScore: Fine-grained Atomic Evaluation of Factual Precision in Long Form Text Generation." *EMNLP 2023.* arXiv:2305.14251. — Introduces decompose-then-verify pipeline: break generation into atomic facts, verify each against knowledge source, compute proportion supported. **Relevance:** Direct methodological precedent for Admittance scoring — decomposing model output into claims and checking each against what is actually visible/determinable from the figure.

54. **CHAIR** — Rohrbach, A., Hendricks, L.A., Burns, K., Darrell, T. & Saenko, K. (2018). "Object Hallucination in Image Captioning." *EMNLP 2018.* arXiv:1809.02156. — Defines CHAIRs (sentence-level) and CHAIRi (instance-level) hallucination rates for image captioning by checking mentioned objects against ground-truth segmentations. **Relevance:** Establishes the paradigm of measuring hallucination rate as a fraction of generated claims that are unsupported by visual evidence — directly analogous to computing Resistance as the fraction of claims that resist adversarial influence.

55. **Measurement to Meaning** — Salaudeen, O., Reuel, A., Ahmed, A., Bedi, S., Robertson, Z., Sundar, S., Domingue, B., Wang, A. & Koyejo, S. (2025). "Measurement to Meaning: A Validity-Centered Framework for AI Evaluation." arXiv:2505.10573. Presented at ICLR 2025. — Provides a psychometrics-grounded framework distinguishing Criterion (directly measurable) from Construct (latent trait), with structured validity facets. **Relevance:** Core theoretical framework for arguing construct validity of Admittance, Resistance, and Inductance as latent constructs operationalized through observable probe responses.

56. **Measuring What Matters** — Burnell, R. et al. (2025). "Measuring what Matters: Construct Validity in Large Language Model Benchmarks." *NeurIPS 2025 Datasets & Benchmarks Track.* arXiv:2511.04703. — Systematic review of 445 LLM benchmarks finding pervasive operationalization weaknesses. Provides eight recommendations and an actionable checklist for construct validity. **Relevance:** Directly applicable checklist for validating that our metric definitions properly operationalize the abstract constructs of admittance, resistance, and inductance.

## Section 9: Calibration & Honesty Metrics

57. **Know Your Limits** — Wen, B. et al. (2024/2025). "Know Your Limits: A Survey of Abstention in Large Language Models." *TACL 2025.* arXiv:2407.18418. — Comprehensive survey organizing abstention literature from query, model, and human-values perspectives. Recommends balancing task performance, abstention performance, coverage, and accuracy-coverage trade-offs (ER, C@Acc, AUROC, AUACC, AURCC). **Relevance:** Provides the theoretical grounding for Admittance as a form of appropriate abstention — models should abstain (admit limitations) when visual information is genuinely degraded or absent.

58. **LLM Honesty Survey** — Li, S. et al. (2025). "A Survey on the Honesty of Large Language Models." *TMLR 2025.* — Defines LLM honesty as requiring both self-knowledge (awareness of own limitations) and self-expression (faithful output of internal knowledge). **Relevance:** Admittance directly measures the self-knowledge dimension of honesty — whether the model knows what it cannot see.

59. **BeHonest** — Chern, S. et al. (2024). "BeHonest: Benchmarking Honesty of Large Language Models." arXiv:2406.13261. — Evaluates three honesty dimensions: awareness of knowledge boundaries (self-knowledge), avoidance of deceit (non-deceptiveness), and consistency. Includes "Expressing Unknowns" and "Admitting Knowns" scenarios. **Relevance:** Our Admittance metric parallels their "Expressing Unknowns" dimension, adapted to the visual domain. Our Resistance metric parallels their non-deceptiveness/sycophancy dimensions.

60. **TruthfulQA** — Lin, S., Hilton, J. & Evans, O. (2022). "TruthfulQA: Measuring How Models Mimic Human Falsehoods." *ACL 2022.* arXiv:2109.07958. — Tests whether models generate false answers that mimic common human misconceptions. Now partially saturated due to training data inclusion. **Relevance:** Establishes the paradigm of testing whether models prioritize truthful responses over plausible-sounding ones — conceptually aligned with Resistance measuring whether models prioritize visual evidence over plausible misleading context.

61. **SimpleQA** — Wei, J. et al. (2024). "Measuring Short-form Factuality in Large Language Models." OpenAI, 2024. arXiv:2411.04368. — Grades responses as correct/incorrect/not-attempted, explicitly allowing abstention. **Relevance:** The three-way grading scheme (correct/incorrect/abstain) directly informs Admittance scoring: a model that appropriately abstains on degraded inputs scores higher than one that fabricates.

62. **Selective Selective Prediction** — Ren, J. et al. (2024). "Selective 'Selective Prediction': Reducing Unnecessary Abstention in Vision-Language Reasoning." *ACL Findings 2024.* arXiv:2402.15610. — Introduces ReCoVERR algorithm to reduce over-abstention in VLMs by collecting additional visual evidence before abstaining. **Relevance:** Highlights that optimal Admittance is not maximal abstention — models should abstain only when genuinely uncertain, not reflexively. Our Inductance metric captures whether models attempt legitimate inference rather than blanket refusal.

## Section 10: Adversarial Robustness & Sycophancy

63. **Towards Understanding Sycophancy** — Sharma, M., Tong, M., Korbak, T., Duvenaud, D., Askell, A., Bowman, S.R. et al. (2024). "Towards Understanding Sycophancy in Language Models." *ICLR 2024.* arXiv:2310.13548. — Demonstrates that RLHF-trained models consistently exhibit sycophancy across free-form tasks, driven by human preference judgments favoring agreeable responses. **Relevance:** Theoretical motivation for Resistance — sycophancy is the failure mode that Resistance quantifies in the visual domain.

64. **SycEval** — Fanous, M. & Goldberg, Y. (2025). "SycEval: Evaluating LLM Sycophancy." arXiv:2502.08177. — Distinguishes progressive sycophancy (leading to correct answers) from regressive sycophancy (leading to incorrect answers). Tests via initial inquiries followed by adversarial rebuttals. **Relevance:** Our Resistance metric specifically measures regressive sycophancy in the visual domain — when misleading context causes the model to abandon correct visual evidence.

65. **Sycophancy under Pressure** — (2025). "Sycophancy under Pressure: Evaluating and Mitigating Sycophantic Bias via Adversarial Dialogues in Scientific QA." arXiv:2508.13743. — Framework capturing sycophantic behavior in single-turn and multi-turn QA; proposes Pressure-Tune as mitigation. **Relevance:** Our Probe B (Resistance-Only) and Probe C (Combined) map directly to their single-turn misleading stance paradigm, adapted to visual scientific figures.

66. **Evidence Grounding Under User Pressure** — (2026). "Evaluating Evidence Grounding Under User Pressure in Instruction-Tuned Language Models." arXiv:2603.20162. — Fine-grained ablations over evidence composition and uncertainty cues across 19 models (0.27B-32B). Finds evidence alone does not reliably prevent user-aligned reversals. **Relevance:** Directly supports our finding that providing misleading captions can override visual evidence even in frontier models. Validates the experimental design of Probe C (degraded visual + misleading context).

67. **ELEPHANT** — (2025). "ELEPHANT: Measuring and Understanding Social Sycophancy in LLMs." arXiv:2505.13995. — Defines social sycophancy as excessive face-preservation; LLMs preserve user face 45pp more than humans. **Relevance:** Provides quantitative precedent for measuring sycophancy rates, analogous to our Resistance scoring as a rate of visual-evidence adherence.

## Section 11: Visual Reasoning from Partial Information

68. **MIRAGE (Inductive Reasoning)** — (2025). "MIRAGE: Evaluating and Explaining Inductive Reasoning Process in Language Models." *ICLR 2025.* arXiv:2410.09542. — Evaluates LLMs on inductive reasoning with meta-rules and meta-facts; finds models rely on neighbor-based reasoning rather than correct rule inference. **Relevance:** Conceptual parallel to Inductance — both measure whether models can infer general patterns from partial observations. Our Inductance metric tests whether models can legitimately infer blurred values from visible chart patterns.

69. **A-I-RAVEN** — (2024). "A-I-RAVEN and I-RAVEN-Mesh: Two New Benchmarks for Abstract Visual Reasoning." arXiv:2406.11061. — Benchmarks abstract visual reasoning with 10 generalization regimes testing rule inference from visual patterns. VLMs perform near random chance. **Relevance:** Demonstrates that visual pattern inference is a genuine capability gap in VLMs, validating Inductance as a meaningful metric that captures a real skill — some models may legitimately infer blurred axis values from data patterns while others cannot.

70. **ConMe** — (2024). "ConMe: Rethinking Evaluation of Compositional Reasoning for Modern VLMs." *NeurIPS 2024 Datasets & Benchmarks Track.* — Automated pipeline for generating hard compositional reasoning questions on arbitrary image sets. **Relevance:** Methodological precedent for generating evaluation probes that test specific reasoning capabilities, analogous to our approach of generating targeted probes from existing figures.

71. **UniBench** — (2024). "UniBench: Visual Reasoning Requires Rethinking Vision-Language Beyond Scaling." *NeurIPS 2024 Datasets & Benchmarks Track.* — Comprehensive VLM evaluation framework categorizing 17 fine-grained capabilities. **Relevance:** Demonstrates that visual reasoning is multi-dimensional — our three metrics (Admittance, Resistance, Inductance) capture three orthogonal dimensions of VLM behavior on scientific figures.

## Section 12: Metric Validation & Statistical Methods

72. **ECE and Calibration** — Naeini, M.P., Cooper, G.F. & Hauskrecht, M. (2015). "Obtaining Well Calibrated Probabilities Using Bayesian Binning into Quantiles." *AAAI 2015.* — Defines Expected Calibration Error (ECE) as the standard metric for measuring calibration of probabilistic predictions. **Relevance:** While ECE measures probabilistic calibration, our Admittance metric measures behavioral calibration — whether model behavior (admitting vs. fabricating) is well-calibrated to the actual information available.

73. **BAS (Behavioral Alignment Score)** — (2026). "A Decision-Theoretic Approach to Evaluating Large Language Model Confidence." arXiv:2604.03216. — Unified decision-theoretic metric that evaluates answer-or-abstain behavior, asymmetrically penalizing highly confident errors. **Relevance:** Provides precedent for asymmetric scoring in abstention metrics. Our Admittance scoring similarly penalizes confident fabrication more than uncertain hedging.

74. **Paired Bootstrap Test** — Efron, B. & Tibshirani, R.J. (1993). *An Introduction to the Bootstrap.* Chapman & Hall. — Foundational reference for bootstrap confidence intervals in metric evaluation. **Relevance:** Recommended method for computing confidence intervals on Admittance, Resistance, and Inductance scores across our 45-figure dataset.

75. **OLAF** — (2025). "OLAF: Operationalization for LLM-based Annotation Framework." — Formalizes annotation as a measurement process with rigorously formalized constructs for reliability, calibration, drift, consensus, aggregation, and transparency. **Relevance:** Provides framework for formalizing our LLM-judge-based scoring of Admittance and Resistance probes as a rigorous measurement process.

76. **Judge's Verdict** — (2025). "Judge's Verdict: A Comprehensive Analysis of LLM Judge Capability Through Human Agreement." arXiv:2510.09738. — Two-step methodology progressing from correlation analysis to Cohen's Kappa analysis for evaluating LLM judges against human judgment. **Relevance:** Methodology for validating our LLM-judge scoring of Admittance/Resistance probes by computing inter-rater agreement (Cohen's Kappa) between LLM judges and human annotators.

77. **LLMs-as-Judges Survey** — (2024). "LLMs-as-Judges: A Comprehensive Survey on LLM-based Evaluation Methods." arXiv:2412.05579. — Comprehensive survey of LLM-based evaluation including agreement metrics (Cohen's/Fleiss' Kappa, percent agreement) for trust calibration. **Relevance:** Establishes best practices for our use of LLM judges (GPT-4o, GPT-5.4-mini, Gemini 2.5 Pro) in scoring metric probes.
